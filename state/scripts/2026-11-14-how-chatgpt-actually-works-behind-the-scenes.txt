[STEP 1: THE INPUT]

Let’s start at the very beginning. You type a prompt and hit enter. What happens next? First, the computer does not read your text like a human does. It does not see words like 'cat' or 'happy'. Instead, it converts those words into numbers. This process is called tokenization. Think of a token as a chunk of text. It could be a whole word, or just part of a word. For example, the word 'chatgpt' might be one token. The phrase 'how are you' might be three tokens.

Every token gets a unique ID number. This is how the computer stores language. Once your sentence is converted into a list of numbers, it is ready for the next stage. This step is crucial. If the tokenization is wrong, the whole answer will be off. But usually, the system handles this smoothly. It breaks down your query into manageable pieces. These numbers are not random; they represent the meaning of the words in a high-dimensional space. But we will get to that analogy later. For now, just know that your sentence is now a sequence of numbers, ready to be processed.

[STEP 2: THE EMBEDDING]

Now, those numbers move into what is called an embedding layer. This is where the math starts to take shape. Imagine a high-dimensional space, often hundreds or thousands of dimensions. In this space, words with similar meanings have small Euclidean vector distances between their coordinate vectors. 'King' and 'Queen' might have a small vector distance. 'King' and 'Apple' would have a large vector distance.

The embedding layer takes your token numbers and turns them into coordinate vectors. It assigns a vector—a list of floating-point numbers—to each token. These vectors are learned parameters, trained via backpropagation to capture the context and the relationship of the word to other words. This is why the AI knows that 'bank' in 'river bank' is mathematically distinct from 'bank' in 'money bank' based on the surrounding token vectors. This step creates a rich, mathematical representation of your intent. It sets the stage for the AI to calculate what you want.

[STEP 3: THE ATTENTION MECHANISM]

This is the core of the model. This is the step most people get wrong. They think the AI processes words one by one, from left to right. But that is not how it works. The AI uses something called 'Self-Attention'.

Here is how it works mathematically. When the AI looks at a word, it generates three vectors: a Query, a Key, and a Value. It calculates the dot product between the Query of the current word and the Key of every other word in your prompt. This produces an unnormalized score. We then apply a scaling factor to stabilize gradients, and a Softmax function across the entire vocabulary to convert these scores into probabilities. These probabilities represent the 'attention scores' for each word in the prompt relative to the current word.

Let’s use an example. If you type 'The bank is full of water', the AI looks at the word 'bank'. It sees 'water' nearby. The dot product between the 'bank' Query and the 'water' Key will be high. The Softmax will assign 'water' a high probability score. But if you type 'The bank is full of money', the AI reassigns that high probability to 'money'. The context changes the focus based on the vector dot products. This happens simultaneously for every word. This mechanism allows the AI to understand long-range dependencies. It can remember the beginning of your sentence while processing the end. This is the core of why ChatGPT feels coherent. It is constantly re-evaluating the relationships between all the words in your prompt using these calculated scores.

[STEP 4: THE TRANSFORMER BLOCKS]

The attention mechanism passes through several layers called Transformer Blocks. Think of these as a series of refinement stages. In each block, the AI refines its understanding using a specific architecture.

First, the input passes through Layer Normalization to stabilize the values. Then, it enters the Multi-Head Attention mechanism. Here, the AI calculates attention scores multiple times simultaneously using different learned projection matrices. This allows the model to attend to different aspects of the language context at once.

After the attention step, the output passes through a Feed-Forward Network. This is a standard neural network with a non-linear activation function, applied independently to each position. This pair of Attention and Feed-Forward is stacked approximately 96 times for a large model like GPT-4. Each pass adds nuance. It is not just 'polishing a stone'; it is a mathematical operation that allows the model to handle complex reasoning and subtle language patterns. The depth of these stacked blocks is what gives the model its power.

[STEP 5: PREDICTION AND SAMPLING]

After all those layers, the AI finally produces an output. But it is not a finished sentence yet. The final layer calculates the probability of every word in its vocabulary coming next.

Here is the critical part. The AI calculates a probability distribution over its entire vocabulary. In a process often called greedy decoding, the AI selects the single most probable word. However, to ensure diversity and avoid repetitive, robotic outputs, the system often uses sampling techniques. For example, 'top-p' (nucleus) sampling restricts the choice to the smallest subset of words whose cumulative probability exceeds p, or 'top-k' sampling restricts the choice to the k most likely words. The system selects randomly from that pool.

Once a word is chosen, it is added to the sentence. Then, the whole process starts all over again. The AI predicts the next word, adds it to the context, and predicts the one after that. This loop continues until the AI predicts a special 'stop' token, or a maximum length limit is reached, signaling that the answer is complete.

[MYTHS AND PRO TIPS]

Let’s talk about common mistakes. A huge myth is that ChatGPT has a brain or consciousness. It does not. It is math. Another myth is that it knows the truth. It does not. It predicts the most likely sequence of tokens based on its training data, not factual reality.

Here is a pro tip. If you want better results, be specific in your prompts. The AI attends to the details you give it. If you give vague prompts, you get vague answers. Another pro tip: use system instructions. Tell the AI to 'think step by step' or 'act as an expert'. This changes the input distribution, which guides the attention mechanism to focus on the relevant parts of the problem.

[RECAP]

To recap what we covered today: We started with tokenization, turning text into unique ID numbers. Then, we looked at embeddings, mapping those numbers to coordinate vectors in a high-dimensional space. The attention mechanism was the star of the show, showing how the AI calculates dot-product-based attention scores between Query, Key, and Value vectors to relate words to each other. We passed through multiple Transformer Blocks, each containing Layer Normalization, Multi-Head Attention, and a Feed-Forward Network, refining the understanding. Finally, the AI calculates a probability distribution over its vocabulary, using sampling techniques like top-p or top-k to select the next word, looping until a stop token is generated.
