First, let's talk about the structure. The roadmap is divided into five stages. Do not try to jump ahead. The first stage focuses on Arrays and Strings. This might seem basic, but trust me, every complex problem you will face later is built on these fundamentals. You will learn two-pointer techniques, sliding windows, and how to reverse a string in place. These are the tools you will use every single day.

Here is the part most people get wrong. They spend too much time on easy problems and get bored, or they jump straight to hard problems and get frustrated. The secret is the 80/20 rule. Spend 80% of your time on the medium difficulty problems in the first three stages. This is where the real skill development happens. The easy problems are just there to build your confidence, but the medium problems force you to think.

Now, let's move to the second stage: Two Pointers and Sliding Window. This is the engine of the roadmap. You will encounter problems like 'Container With Most Water' and 'Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters'. These problems teach you how to optimize your code to scan input linearly. Imagine trying to find a specific card in a deck of 52 cards by checking every possible pair versus using two pointers to scan the deck once. The latter is how you reduce time complexity from O(n^2) to O(n). That is a massive leap in efficiency.

But here is the mistake I see constantly. Viewers try to memorize the solution to a specific problem, like 'Container With Most Water'. They learn the code, they get the points, but when the interviewer changes the problem slightly, they freeze. Do not memorize solutions. Master the pattern. Understand *why* the two-pointer approach works. Ask yourself, 'What is the invariant? What is moving left and what is moving right?' If you understand the logic, you can adapt to any variation the interviewer throws at you.

Stay with me because next, we are going to cover the third stage, which is where most people quit. We are talking about Trees and Graphs. For many, this is the scary part. Binary Trees, Binary Search Trees, and Graph traversals like BFS and DFS. But I am going to give you an everyday analogy that makes this click. Imagine a family tree. You have ancestors, descendants, and you want to navigate the connections between relatives. That is essentially what a graph problem is asking you to do. We will break down how to implement BFS using a queue and DFS using recursion or a stack. It is simpler than it looks, but you have to practice the patterns.

Next up is the fourth stage: Dynamic Programming. This is the beast. Problems like 'Climbing Stairs' or 'Maximum Subarray'. The myth is that you need to be a math genius to solve these. Not true. You need to recognize the pattern. The pattern is simple: if you can solve a problem for a smaller input, can you use that solution to solve the larger input? That is the essence of Dynamic Programming. We will cover the tabulation method versus the memoization method. Knowing when to use which is a pro tip that will save you time during an interview.

The final stage, Stage Five, is the capstone. This is where you combine everything you have learned. You will face problems that require knowledge of Arrays, Trees, and Dynamic Programming all in one. These are the problems hiring managers love to ask because they test your ability to synthesize information. Do not be discouraged if these feel hard. Even senior engineers find these challenging. The key is to break the problem down into the stages we just covered.

Before we wrap up, let's talk about the biggest myth. Many of you think you need to solve all 150 problems to get hired. False. Quality over quantity. It is better to solve 50 problems thoroughly, understanding every edge case and time complexity, than to glance at 150 and remember nothing. Focus on understanding the patterns. If you can explain your thought process out loud, you have already won half the battle.

So, here is your action plan. Do not try to solve them in alphabetical order. Follow the roadmap sequence. Start with Arrays, master Two Pointers, conquer Trees and Graphs, tackle Dynamic Programming, and finish with the capstone problems. Use the 'discuss' section on LeetCode to see how others approached the problems, but do not copy-paste. Write your own solution, even if it is brute force first, and then optimize it.
